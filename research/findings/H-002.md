# H-002 — cross-strike no-arbitrage violations (threshold monotonicity)

- **evaluated:** 2026-09-19 (read-only archive study, no live exposure)
- **data:** `/opt/kbe/archive/signals_*.parquet`, 42 daily files, 2026-08-05 → 2026-09-15
- **scope tested:** monotonicity leg only — `contract_type == 'threshold'`, same event,
  `ask(X1) < bid(X2)` for `X1 < X2`. The bracket-sum and threshold-difference
  identities in the registry entry were **not** tested.

## VERDICT — **REFUTED**

Not "a small edge we cannot resolve." The violations are non-executable by
construction, and the largest one in the corpus is provably a data artifact.

Over 42 days and 18.3M threshold observations the entire population of
violations that (a) clear fees and (b) have any size on both legs is **21
pair-instances worth $0.79 total at one contract each**. Twenty of those 21 have
a stale book on at least one leg. The single instance with both legs on a clean,
fresh book nets **exactly $0.00**.

---

## Filters applied, and resulting n

| step | n |
|---|---|
| threshold rows, valid strike + bid/ask, deduped to one row per (event, scan-cycle, strike) | **18,296,460** |
| scan-cycle clusters (an "instant") | 730,100 |
| clusters with ≥2 strikes quoted — the denominator for "opportunity" | **658,958** |
| observations passing book filter (`0 < spread ≤ 0.05` **and** `book_age_ms < 2000`) | 1,824,210 (**9.97%**) |
| book-clean clusters with ≥2 strikes | **108,372** |

**Event key.** I grouped by the ticker prefix before `-T` (e.g. `KXBTCD-26SEP1617`),
not by `(underlying, close_time)`. This pins series + date + settlement hour and is
strictly stricter than the spec, which removes one way to manufacture fake pairs.

**"Same instant."** `ts` is unique per row, so instants had to be reconstructed.
The engine emits in bursts: all strikes of one event are written within ~0.3s of a
single shared book fetch, bursts ~34s apart. I cluster on a 2s gap. Verified: max
`|ts_buy − ts_sell|` across all violations found = **2.10s**, p99 = **0.81s**, and
`book_age_ms` differs by <5ms between legs of the same cluster — i.e. both legs
genuinely come from one book snapshot.

**Pairing method.** Within a cluster sorted ascending by strike, for each strike `j`
I take the prefix-minimum ask over all strikes below it. Violation iff that ask
< `bid(j)`. This finds the *maximum-gross* violating pair for each sell leg. It
reports one pair per sell leg rather than all pairs (conservative on frequency),
but it never under-states the best available edge — which is the measurement that
fails.

## 2. Magnitude after costs

Fee `max(0.01, ceil(0.07·p(1−p)·100)/100)` per contract per leg. The **1-cent
minimum applies to both legs**, so the round trip costs ≥ **$0.02**. Prices live on
a 1-cent grid. Therefore a violation must be ≥ **3 cents gross** to net anything —
and the observed distribution is concentrated at exactly 1 cent.

| book-filtered violations (n=363) | value |
|---|---|
| gross, median / p90 / mean | **$0.01** / $0.01 / $0.0128 |
| net, median / p90 | **−$0.01** / −$0.01 |
| net > 0 | **5 of 363 (1.4%)** — and 3 of the 5 net exactly $0.00 |
| total net if all 363 taken at size 1 | **−$3.27** |

Raw candidates (n=1,343): 77 net>0 (5.7%); total at size 1 = **−$4.62**.

---

## 3. Persistence — high, and it argues *against* the hypothesis

| horizon | both legs requoted | violation still present | still net>0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| t+1 (median 33s) | 356 / 363 | **316 (88.8%)** | 1 |
| t+2 (67s) | 348 | 297 (85.3%) | 0 |
| t+3 (101s) | 345 | 286 (82.9%) | 1 |
| t+4 (135s) | 341 | 272 (79.8%) | 0 |

(Raw candidates decay faster: 72.4% → 57.0% over the same span.)

**Read this the opposite way to the obvious one.** The pre-registered worry was
that violations evaporate faster than a 32s loop can act. They do not — and that is
worse news, not better. A genuine riskless arbitrage sitting untouched on a live
exchange for **over two minutes** is not credible. High persistence is the
signature of a quote *nobody can trade against*, which measurement 4 confirms
directly. And it does not rescue the economics: at t+1 exactly **one** of 356
persisting violations still cleared costs.

## 4. Size available — the mechanism of the refutation

| book-filtered violations | value |
|---|---|
| median min-leg size | **0** |
| instances with min-leg size = 0 | **290 of 363 (80%)** |

Across all 1,343 raw violations, against corpus base rates:

| | in violations | base rate (ask<1.0 rows) | enrichment |
|---|---|---|---|
| buy-leg `ask_size == 0` (nothing offered) | **42.7%** | 1.6% | **26×** |
| sell-leg `bid_size == 0` (no one bidding) | **13.5%** | 2.2% | **6×** |
| either leg zero size | **56.2%** | — | — |

**This is real reported depth, not missing data.** I verified across three sampled
days (531k rows with `ask < 1.0`) that `ask_size` is **never NaN when `ask < 1.0`**.
`ask = 1.00` with `ask_size = NaN` means *no offer exists*; such rows can never be a
buy leg anyway, since a violation requires `buy_ask < sell_bid ≤ 1.0`. So the
zero-size readings are the exchange reporting a price with no contracts behind it.

### The final tradeable set: net>0 **and** min-leg size ≥ 1

**21 instances over 42 days, $0.79 total at one contract each.** Twenty of the 21
fail the book filter on at least one leg. The three largest (2026-09-03,
`KXBTCD-26SEP0306`, $0.50 of the $0.79) sit on books **26 seconds stale** — the
textbook one-leg-stale artifact.

The only instance with both legs clean and fresh:
`2026-08-21 KXBTCD-26AUG2117`, buy 75099.99 @ $0.89, sell 75199.99 @ $0.91,
size 1, gross $0.02, **net $0.0000**.

## Robustness

### Sensitivity to the book filter (independent re-implementation, 19.5M rows)

The strict 2s staleness cut is not what is hiding an edge — relaxing it adds
nothing that survives the size requirement:

| filter | clusters | violations | net>0 | net>0 & size≥1 | net>0 & size≥10 | total $ (size≥1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spread≤5c & age<2s (**primary**) | 150,939 | 516 | 25 | **1** | 0 | **$0.00** |
| spread≤5c & age<5s | 341,032 | 1,075 | 59 | 12 | 11 | $0.18 |
| spread≤5c & age<10s | 418,747 | 1,235 | 65 | 14 | 13 | $0.19 |
| spread≤5c, any age | 713,911 | 1,661 | 139 | 19 | 17 | $0.72 |
| primary + depth on both sides | 126,707 | 73 | 1 | 1 | 0 | $0.00 |
| **no filter at all** | 770,468 | 1,820 | 171 | **21** | 17 | **$0.79** |

Every path leads to under a dollar over six weeks.

### Day-level significance and jackknife

Aggregated to daily totals, tested across 42 days. Per-observation statistics are
not reported — violations cluster in 21 events and would be wildly inflated.

**Tradeable set (net>0 & size≥1), the only economically meaningful series:**

| | mean/day | t | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 42 days | $0.0188 | 1.55 | $0.79 |
| drop best 1 | $0.0071 | 2.18 | $0.29 |
| drop best 2 | $0.0048 | 2.05 | $0.19 |
| drop best 3 | $0.0031 | 1.87 | $0.12 |

**The rising t-stat under jackknife is an artifact, not a strength.** With only
8 nonzero days out of 42, removing the one large day shrinks the variance faster
than the mean. Read the total column instead: it falls from $0.79 to $0.12. At
these magnitudes the t-statistic is measuring nothing.

Book-filtered set (ignoring size): mean $0.0207/day, t=1.00, total $0.87 — **all
of it from 2026-09-12**; drop that day and the total is **$0.00** across the
remaining 41 days.

Raw set (ignoring size and book quality): mean $0.268/day, t=1.12, total $11.25 —
of which **$10.03 is 2026-09-12 alone**. That day is dissected below and is not
real.

### The largest violation in the corpus is a data-integrity artifact

The top raw violations (gross **$0.71**) are all `KXETHD-26SEP1208` on 2026-09-12,
20 minutes from a 12:00 UTC close. Direct archive lookup of that single scan cycle
shows **two interleaved, mutually inconsistent strike ladders under one event**:

- a **$15-step** ladder (2309.99, 2324.99, 2339.99 … 2429.99, 2434.99) bidding
  **0.91–0.92**, `bid_size` 5–40, `book_age ≈ 1895ms`
- a **$5-step** ladder (2314.99, 2319.99, 2329.99 … 2419.99) bidding **0.20–0.55**,
  `bid_size ≈ 800`, `book_age ≈ 2060ms`

Bids are grossly non-monotone in strike — 0.20 bid at 2419.99 against 0.91 bid at
2429.99 — which is not a market, it is a join error. Every contract in that window
settled **YES** (ETH finished above 2489.99), so a true book 20 minutes out could
not bid 0.20 for the 2424.99 YES while bidding 0.92 for the 2489.99 YES. And the
buy leg at the centre of it (2424.99, ask 0.21) carries **`ask_size = 0`**.

This is exactly the "the two contracts are not actually on the same settlement
event" failure mode, and it is the single largest number in the study.

## How an apparent edge here would be an artifact — all three confirmed present

1. **Stale quote on one leg.** 73% of raw candidates die on this. In the final
   21-instance tradeable set, 20 have a stale leg, and the three biggest are on
   26-second-old books.
2. **Not the same settlement event / mis-joined quotes.** Confirmed on 2026-09-12
   ETH, which alone drives 89% of the raw daily EV.
3. **Size unavailable.** 80% of book-clean violations have zero size on a leg;
   `ask_size == 0` is 26× enriched over base rate. The "arbitrage" is buying where
   nothing is offered.

## Limitations — stated plainly

- **This is a signal log, not a book tape.** A strike appears only when the engine
  emitted a signal for it, at ~32–35s cadence. Violations living and dying inside
  30s are structurally invisible. The persistence result argues the observed
  population is not dominated by fast decay, but it cannot exclude a fast
  population the sampling never sees. Settling that needs a real websocket book
  tape, which this dataset is not.
- **The book-clean subsample is selected by engine load, not market state.**
  `book_age < 2000ms` keeps only 9.97% of rows; median book age is 7.7s and one
  entire day (2026-09-13) has **zero** qualifying observations. The clean 10% is
  "when the poll loop happened to be fast," not a random sample of conditions.
- **Effective sample is far smaller than n suggests.** 42 days, 21 distinct events,
  8 nonzero days in the tradeable series.
- **Top-of-book only, and leg risk is unmodelled.** Even where size > 0, filling
  one leg and missing the other converts a riskless arb into a naked directional
  position — a cost this study does not charge.

## What "REFUTED" does and does not cover

Refuted **for the monotonicity leg, at this data source and sampling rate**. The
economics are what make this robust to the sampling caveat: a 2-cent fee floor
against a 1-cent tick means any real opportunity must be ≥3c gross, i.e. a large
dislocation that would be visible at 32s sampling if it occurred with any
regularity. It does not occur.

**Not tested, and still open:** the bracket-sum identity (non-overlapping brackets
summing to ~1) and the threshold-difference identity
(`P(above X) − P(above Y) = P(bracket X–Y)`). Those involve `contract_type ==
'bracket'`, which is 7.7% of the corpus and was excluded here. They would need
their own registration. Given that brackets are already a known error source in the
engine, and that the 2-cent fee floor applies there too with the same 1-cent tick,
the prior should be low.

**Recommendation:** do not register H-002 for live or paper-forward evaluation in
its monotonicity form. Do not spend a strategy slot on it.
