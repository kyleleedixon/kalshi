## 4. Two things worth a human's attention

**a. The loop never came back to where H-003's writeup says it did.** That
writeup records maker-first being disabled 09-14 03:03 with the loop recovering
to 34s. Six days later STATE shows cadence 57-65s and median book age 26.3s —
essentially the 26.8s the writeup attributed to maker-first. So either the
recovery did not hold or the 09-14 attribution was incomplete; something is
holding the loop above 57s that is not maker-first execution. I cannot diagnose
it here: `ops/publish.py` and the live loop timings are not in this checkout.

Note one arithmetic coincidence before treating this as two faults: 26.3s is
close to half a 57-65s loop period, which is what median book age would read if
it were sampled across the loop rather than at the decision. If that is what
the monitor measures, there is one problem (loop cadence) and not two, and the
26s figure says little about freshness at the moment an order is priced — the
quantity H-001's rule was actually about. Resolving which quantity
`book_staleness` reports is the single most useful next step, and it is a
code-reading question, not a data question.

**b. A confound in the refuted sample, recorded here for the next design only.**
81% of the pooled H-001 sample (the 470 `_maker_v2` contracts) was taken while
median book age was 26.8s per H-003's own writeup — violating the <2s
precondition that H-001's claim is *about*. The registry treats 221s-stale books
as grounds for discarding a window (the 09-11 clean-window note), and this is
the same category of problem.

This does **not** reopen H-001. Reconsidering a kill rule after seeing the
results is precisely what Rule 3 exists to prevent, and I am not proposing a
re-run or a search for a better filter on the same data. It matters only
prospectively: whatever is registered next should enforce book freshness at the
gate and refuse to trade when it is not met, rather than assuming the condition
holds. Per (a), it is not met today.
