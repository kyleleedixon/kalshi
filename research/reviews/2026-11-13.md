### (c) Correction to this channel's own information set: `book_age_ms` is a loop-duration metric

`.claude/agents/kbe-ops.md` (present in the repo since the oldest commit in reach — **this is not a
change in engine state, it is something prior reviews here did not use**) states:

> **Book staleness tracks loop speed.** `book_age_ms` measures the gap between parsing a market row
> and writing its signal, so it is really a loop-duration metric.

Three consequences, and they matter more than the number falling:

1. **Cadence does not independently corroborate the staleness median.** They are substantially the
   same measurement. Today's two headline improvements are **one** observation, not two, and the
   "never treat a single number as fact" rule therefore still bites. The median falling and the
   cadence falling together is what one metric looks like, not confirmation.
2. **A green `book_staleness` is not evidence that H-001's `book < 2s` precondition is satisfiable.**
   H-001's rule is about book data age *relative to the decision*; `book_age_ms` is parse→write
   duration inside a cycle. The engine's own trading-time freshness gate is a different instrument
   (`StalenessReason.FRESH` on the oracle estimate, `engine/.../core/estimate.py`), and it is not
   published in STATE at all. **Anyone reasoning from the monitor to the entry rule is making a
   measurement error.** This is the specific way today's good news could be misused.
3. It also partly answers **q11** ("can `book_staleness` go green by going quiet"): if the metric is
   intra-cycle duration, then yes — a loop that emits little can post a small gap. The minimum
   signal-count guard proposed in q11 is still the right ask and is **not** assumed applied.

q2 and q13 are accordingly **narrowed, not closed**: what is still missing from STATE is the signal
count beside the median and the monitor source, not the definition.
