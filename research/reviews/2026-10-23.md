10-21's **falsifiable 1** (*a BLIND-free 24 h window with max staleness under ~40,000 ms and

satisfied**, so 10-21 §1(a) stands. The window does contain a BLIND-free run of 29 publishes

threshold — but its cadence maxima include 3,868 s, 1,078 s, 929 s, 545 s and 464 s.

**This is where I correct 10-21, not extend it.** 10-21 §1(b) read the publish-level divergence
of the two statistics as evidence that they measure different things. Given §1(b) above, the
simpler reading is that they measure the **same** thing — loop duration — through different
estimators: staleness is a **median** over an undefined "recent log" window, cadence is a
**maximum** over the last 5 cycles. A median shrugs off exactly the outlier cycles a maximum is
built to catch, so a run can show a recovered median alongside 3,868 s maxima with no second
fault required. **One fault, two estimators** fits the documented mechanism and needs no
appeal to decoupling.

That demotes "two separable faults" to unsupported, and promotes **q3** (the undefined window
width) from tidiness to the item blocking interpretation — without it, the median cannot be
compared to anything. It does not soften §1(c)'s facts: a 6,534 s cycle and a BLIND episode
3 h ago are the fault, whichever estimator sees them.
